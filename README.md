# Customer Churn Analysis & Retention Strategy
**Python | SQL | Power BI** - an end-to-end data analytics portfolio project

> **One-line summary:** 24% of telecom customers churn, costing 27% of monthly revenue. Contract type, Fiber optic service, early tenure and support tickets drive it. A calibrated model (ROC-AUC 0.81) ranks active customers by risk, and a profit-optimised campaign contacting the top 20% beats contacting everyone (+INR 128K vs -INR 83K on the test set).

![Churn by category](images/01_churn_by_category.png)

## 1. Business problem
A telecom operator is losing about one customer in four and spends retention effort without targeting. Questions: why do customers leave, who is next, and whom should we contact? Full brief: [`docs/Business_Problem_Document.pdf`](docs/Business_Problem_Document.pdf).

## 2. Dataset
**Synthetic** (generated by `src/generate_data.py`, fixed seed) with realistic churn drivers and deliberate data problems. 7,260 raw rows -> **7,200 customers**, 26 columns (profile, contract, services, billing, support tickets, churn flag). Currency INR.

## 5. Method
1. **Clean**: 60 duplicates, 40 negative charges, 144 missing total charges, 108 missing gender, inconsistent text - all logged.
2. **Explore**: churn rate by attribute, chi-square significance, effect size.
3. **Model**: 3 models, 5-fold CV on training data, one final evaluation on a 25% test set.
4. **Decide**: threshold from expected campaign profit; calibration verified.
5. **Act**: out-of-fold risk score for all customers, risk tiers, retention playbooks.

## 6. Key findings
| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Churn is **24.2%** (1,741 of 7,200); churned customers carried **INR 16.3 M annualised revenue (27% of monthly revenue)** | SQL Q1 |
| 2 | **Contract is the top driver:** month-to-month churn 40.9% vs one-year 15.9% vs two-year 7.6%; month-to-month customers are 73% of all churners | SQL Q2 |
| 3 | **Month-to-month + Fiber optic** is 18% of customers but **43% of churners** (56% churn rate; 72% if in first year) | SQL Q4, Q15 |
| 4 | **Early tenure is dangerous:** 0-6 months 43.9% churn vs 49+ months 11.7% | SQL Q3 |
| 5 | **Service pain:** 3+ support tickets in 6 months -> 41% churn vs 15% with none | SQL Q7 |
| 6 | Electronic-check payers churn 29% vs 21% for credit-card payers; customers without tech support churn 31% | notebook s4 |
| 7 | Region has **no significant effect** (p = 0.09); add-on count alone barely matters - the *type* of customer and service quality matter more | notebook s4, SQL Q5 |
| 8 | Model: logistic regression best (CV AUC 0.822, **test ROC-AUC 0.806**, PR-AUC 0.579 vs 0.24 random); simpler model beat random forest (0.791) and gradient boosting (0.800) | notebook s6 |
| 9 | Probabilities are **well calibrated**; the top risk decile churns at ~70% (2.9x lift); top 3 deciles capture ~63% of churners | notebook s8, SQL Q12 |
| 10 | Profit-optimal threshold 0.43 -> contact **20% of customers**: recall 50%, precision 60%, expected profit **INR 128K** vs **-INR 83K** if everyone is contacted (test set) | notebook s8 |
| 11 | **616 active customers** (11%) are above the threshold, with **INR 6.1 M annualised revenue at risk**; 91% are month-to-month, 71% Fiber optic | notebook s9, SQL Q10 |

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Convert month-to-month customers to 12-month contracts** with a price lock (largest group in the action list).
2. **Fix the first year:** onboarding call at month 1-2, first-year contract discount for new Fiber customers.
3. **Act on service pain:** trigger a technical visit when a customer reaches 3 tickets in 6 months.
4. **Move electronic-check payers to auto-pay** with a small bill credit; offer free tech-support trials to unprotected internet customers.
5. **Contact only customers above the profit-optimal threshold** and run a pilot with a control group to measure the *real* offer success rate; update the assumptions (cost INR 400, success 30%, margin 40%) and re-run.

## 9. Limitations
Synthetic data; snapshot churn label (no time-to-event); campaign economics are assumptions; correlation is not causation; no A/B test of offers.
